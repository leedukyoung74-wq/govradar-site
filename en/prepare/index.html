Be preparedStockpile kit

72-hour basic kit

  • Water: about 4 L (1 gallon) per person per day, at least 3 days (2 weeks if possible)
  • No-cook food for 3 days to 2 weeks, manual can opener
  • Two weeks of prescription and basic medicines, first-aid supplies
  • Flashlight or lantern, spare batteries
  • Battery or hand-crank radio
  • Power bank and charging cables
  • Some cash in case card payments stop
  • Copies of ID, insurance and bank documents in a waterproof bag
  • Masks, hygiene items, blankets and warm clothes, whistle
  • A family contact plan and meeting point

War · armed conflict

  • Know your nearest public shelter
  • Learn the alert types: alert, air raid, CBRN
  • CBRN basics: gas mask or wet towel, plastic sheeting and tape to seal windows
  • Keep your fuel tank at least half full
  • Abroad: register with your embassy, check passport validity
  • Business: insure receivables in conflict-risk markets, plan staff evacuation, line up alternative suppliers

Famine · food crisis

  • Rotating 2-week to 3-month stock: rice and grains, canned food, dried food, cooking oil, salt and sugar
  • Eat the oldest first and refill (rotate by expiry)
  • Water filter or purification tablets
  • No panic buying — build up gradually in normal times
  • Business: watch the FAO Food Price Index and GIEWS alerts, diversify ingredient suppliers

Pandemic

  • Respirator masks, hand sanitiser, thermometer
  • One to three months of regular medicines
  • Check vaccinations
  • Test remote work and video meetings in advance
  • Follow health-ministry and WHO outbreak notices
  • Business: backup rota for key staff, remote-work continuity plan

Economic crisis · depression

  • Emergency fund for 3 to 6 months of living costs
  • Pay down high-interest debt first
  • Spread deposits within each bank's deposit-insurance limit
  • Hold some cash
  • Diversify income and customers, cut fixed costs
  • Business: track stimulus, infrastructure and jobs programmes on the radar — public spending rises in downturns
  • General information, not investment advice

Blackout · cyber outage

  • Flashlights, lanterns, power banks, solar charger
  • Battery radio
  • Camping stove and fuel (ventilate indoors)
  • Cash in case cards and mobile payments stop
  • Keep the fridge and freezer closed (fridge stays cold about 4 hours)
  • Avoid lifts during outages
  • Key contacts on paper, important files backed up offline
  • Business: UPS or generator, offline backups, cyber-security check

Earthquake · tsunami · storm · volcano

  • Turn on emergency alerts on your phone
  • Earthquake: drop under a table; when it stops, take the stairs to open ground
  • Tsunami: if warned on the coast, or the sea suddenly recedes, go to high ground at once
  • Storms and floods: secure windows, stay away from rivers and coasts
  • Volcanic ash: mask and goggles, stay indoors
  • Secure furniture, know your evacuation route

Sources: US FEMA, Seoul safety portal, Korea disaster portal, Korea MOFA travel safety

GOV RADAR

Home stockpile kit — what, how much, which products

This kit follows official stockpile guidance from national governments (Korea's disaster-safety portal, US FEMA and CDC, UK Be Ready, Dutch Denk Vooruit, Japan's Disaster Management Agency). Prepare a little at a time and rotate stock — not panic buying.
Quantities are planning figures for adults; adjust for health and storage space.

Stage 1 · 72 hours

Secure water, no-cook food, medicines, a torch and a power bank first.

Stage 2 · 2 weeks

Buy a little more of what you normally eat and rotate stock — eat the oldest first.

Stage 3 · 1 month +

Decide by household, storage space, cost and real risk. There is no basis for buying in bulk beyond need.

Safety first

  • Water here is 3 L per person per day; the American Red Cross advises 1 gallon (about 3.8 L), so add more if you can.
  • Frozen food alone is fragile in a blackout — include shelf-stable, no-cook food.
  • Charcoal, generators or camping stoves indoors risk carbon-monoxide poisoning.
  • Get prescription medicines only within what your prescriber and pharmacist advise.
  • Food quantities are examples that vary with diet and health and do not guarantee complete nutrition.
