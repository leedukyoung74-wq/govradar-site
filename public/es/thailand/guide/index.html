GOV RADAR / Tailandia

How to sell to the Government of Thailand

Verificado el 2026-10-31

Procurement is decentralised across central and provincial agencies and state enterprises. Foreign firms usually need a reputable local representative to bid effectively; agents are accepted as legitimate bidders, and firms may bid without one if the agency allows. Large international tenders may admit foreign firms, but smaller contracts typically require Thai registration and local presence.

Portales de adquisiciones

  • e-GP (Thai Government Procurement)

Cómo participar

  1. Appoint a local agent or partner; they give early notice of tenders and check spec compliance.
  2. Register as a supplier on e-GP with Thai business credentials.
  3. Respond to RFIs or Rough Order of Magnitude requests ahead of formal tenders.
  4. Check whether a two-envelope (technical/price) system applies.
  5. Include training costs in the bid.
  6. Appeals must be filed via e-GP within 7 working days of the result (mandatory from 1 Oct 2026, cutoff 16:30).

Preferencia local y límites

  • SMEs get a 10% price preference in competitive bidding.
  • Thailand is not a party to the WTO Government Procurement Agreement.
  • Agencies may favour domestic suppliers they already work with.
  • Offsets, technology transfer, training and after-sales service are evaluation factors.

Idioma de la oferta · Mainly Thai; the e-GP platform is primarily in Thai.

Proyectos de bancos de desarrollo

Many public works are financed by multilateral development banks, notably the Asian Development Bank; follow the bank's procurement rules.

Conviene saber

  • Agencies need not accept the lowest bid and may change technical requirements or call rebids.
  • Integrity Pacts are mandatory on major contracts, with public observers monitoring.
  • Foreign entities with no legal representative in Thailand are excluded from the e-appeal guidelines; other review rights remain.
  • Key sectors: aviation/defence, power, renewables, petrochemicals, telecom, transport, ICT, environment, health.
  • A Green Public Procurement framework launched in 2026 may favour green products.
