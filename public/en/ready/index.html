What to do first when danger is now

This page is not a live alert. If you are in danger, follow your local authority's alerts and evacuation orders first. In Korea: fire, rescue and ambulance 119, police 112, and the Korea Safety Portal. In other countries, follow that country's official disaster agency.

Actions and preparation by hazard

War, air raids, long disruption

On an alert, go to a designated shelter or solid indoor space. Check routes from home, work and school in advance, and avoid basements that can flood.

30 days72-hour bag, 30-day rotating food, walk the evacuation route
60 daysTemporary housing, transport, water and prescription refills in a safe area
90 daysSplit living, income loss, childcare and schooling plans
1 yearDocument renewal, long-term housing, health and livelihood recovery, regular drills

Ordinary dust masks do not stop chemical gases. Follow the local response agency, since actions depend on the chemical, wind and terrain.

[2] Korea — emergency and air-raid actions

Famine, food supply disruption

Famine is a crisis of food, water, health and income that home stocks alone cannot solve. Find public meals, rations and welfare services before food runs out.

30 daysTry a 7-day menu; set 30-day rotating stock and living costs
60 daysTwo or more suppliers, public aid eligibility, safe transport
90 daysCheck 60–90 days of balanced food, cooking water and storage
1 yearAnnual food budget, income replacement, local supply cooperation

Unknown wild plants or mushrooms, mouldy grain and over-diluted formula are not solutions. People who have not eaten for long need medical assessment before large meals.

[20] WFP — understanding famine

Epidemics

No single method fits every disease. Prepare by route (air and contact, water and hygiene, mosquitoes) and follow current health-authority guidance on isolation, vaccines and treatment.

30 daysHome care plan, safe ventilation, food delivery, steady medicines
60 daysCarer shifts, home life, income loss and resupply routes
90 daysFilters, hygiene supplies, carer load, missed check-ups
1 yearRe-assess vaccines, chronic care and recovery plans

Do not self-medicate with antibiotics, antivirals or disinfectants. Replace masks by condition and contamination, not by a fixed number of days.

[9] CDC — respiratory virus prevention [10] CDC — respiratory viruses and air quality

Volcanoes, ash

In pyroclastic-flow, lava or lahar zones, evacuation comes first. Where ash falls, protect indoor air, and check lahar maps along valleys and rivers.

30 daysRoutes, protective gear, first indoor supplies
60 daysVehicle and ventilation filters, alternative housing
90 daysWater, power, housing and care during prolonged ash
1 yearRe-assess living in the zone, building condition, livelihood

N95 masks filter particles, not volcanic gas. Do not climb onto roofs to clear ash; ask experts about load and collapse risk.

[14] USGS — volcanic ash protection [15] USGS — prepare for volcanic activity

Earthquakes, aftershocks

While it shakes, drop, cover your head and neck and hold on. When it stops, check damage, fire, gas, tsunami risk at the coast and evacuation orders.

30 daysSecure furniture, family drill, bag
60 daysWater and power cuts, temporary housing
90 daysDamage checks, repairs, medical and insurance records
1 yearBuilding safety review, drills, insurance terms

Do not rely on doorframes or the 'triangle of life' claim.

[11] CDC — during an earthquake [12] CDC — earthquake preparedness

Tsunamis

Strong or long shaking at the coast, a sudden sea change or a roar from the ocean are natural warnings. After protecting yourself, go to high ground or inland without waiting for an alert.

30 daysWalk the route; adjust bag location
60 daysPractise blocked roads, night, separated family
90 daysShelter living, documents, insurance, housing recovery
1 yearRe-check maps and routes when travelling or moving

Do not return after the first wave. Wait for the all-clear, and avoid plans that depend on driving through traffic.

[13] NOAA — during a tsunami

Wildfires, smoke

If fire approaches and you are told to leave, leave early. If only smoke comes in and the building is safe, set up a clean-air room; move if it gets too hot or breathing worsens.

30 daysBag, clean-air room, backup lodging
60 daysFilters, supplies, power and cooling options
90 daysAdjust housing, school, work and care in long smoke
1 yearBefore fire season: routes, surroundings, insurance

Air purifiers and N95 masks do not supply oxygen or remove carbon monoxide. Never stay because of indoor filtration when fire approaches.

[16] AirNow — be smoke ready [17] AirNow — when smoke is in the air

Comets, asteroids, meteors

No personal gear can stop an impact. Rely on NASA, recognised astronomers and local agencies, and apply blast, fire, tsunami or outage actions as advised. This page predicts no impact.

30 daysKnow official sources; finish basic preparedness
60 daysCheck misinformation steps and family contacts
90 daysLodging, health and documents for wide-area moves
1 yearUpdate the general plan; avoid unfounded overbuying

No one should calculate impact dates or claim a year's stock guarantees survival.

[21] NASA — planetary defense [22] NASA CNEOS — impact exercises

Nuclear, radioactive fallout

Get inside the nearest solid building, in a basement or the centre away from walls and roof, and listen for official instructions. If you were outside, remove outer clothing into a bag and wash exposed skin.

30 daysIndoor shelter space, radio, basic bag
60 daysFamily contact plan, minimise outdoor time
90 daysLodging, health and documents for long sheltering
1 yearRe-check official channels and drills

CDC's 'stay inside at least 24 hours' is general protection, not an all-clear. Particle masks do not block external radiation.

[19] CDC — radiation emergencies

Infants, older people, disability, chronic illness and pets need individual plans. Discuss spare prescriptions, cold-chain medicines and backup power for medical devices with a clinician. [6] CDC — prescriptions [7] FDA — safe drug use after a disaster [8] CDC — infant feeding in emergencies
