The order of preparation in 30 seconds

You can start today in 30 minutes: contact card and shelters first, then a 3-day bag, 14- and 30-day stock, and 60/90-day and yearly checks.

What to do first when danger is now

This page is not a live alert. If you are in danger, follow your local authority's alerts and evacuation orders first. In Korea: fire, rescue and ambulance 119, police 112, and the Korea Safety Portal. In other countries, follow that country's official disaster agency.

Tools for when the internet and phone networks fail

Install, download and test these before a disaster. Once networks are down you cannot download apps or maps.

These tools do not replace emergency services (119/112 in Korea, your local number elsewhere) or official alerts. Follow official alerts and evacuation orders first.

Organic Maps WMM rating: High

WhatMaps and directions without the internet
DevicesAndroid · iPhone (iOS 15+) · Mac · Linux — no Windows
You needA phone and maps downloaded in advance
Works offlineGPS position (satellite — weak indoors and underground), search within downloaded areas, walking and driving routes
Does not work offlineDownloading new areas or updates, live traffic or road closures, whether a shelter is open right now
RangeNo range limit (inside downloaded areas)
BatteryNavigation uses GPS and screen heavily — carry a power bank
CostFree · open source (Apache-2.0) · OpenStreetMap data

Before a disaster

  1. Install the app and download the maps for where you live and may need to go.
  2. Save home, work, school and the nearest shelter as bookmarks.
  3. In airplane mode, route once from home to the shelter.

Briar WMM rating: High · set up in advance

WhatText messages with people nearby, without the internet
DevicesAndroid · desktop for Windows/Mac/Linux — no iPhone
You needBriar on both sides and contacts added in advance
Works offlineEncrypted texts with added contacts nearby (Bluetooth, same Wi-Fi)
Does not work offlinePeople far away (needs internet/Tor), iPhone users, anyone not added, voice calls
RangeBluetooth range — usually tens of metres, under 100 m even in the open; walls cut it sharply
BatteryKeeping Bluetooth/Wi-Fi on drains the battery faster
CostFree · open source (GPL-3.0) · external security audit in 2017

Before a disaster

  1. Meet family and colleagues in person and add each other in the app (adding from afar is hard).
  2. Turn on Bluetooth/Wi-Fi sharing and test in the same room and through a wall.
  3. Agree another channel for anyone using an iPhone.

  1. Install the app and download what you need (e.g. Wikipedia in your language, medical wiki, travel guides).
  2. Check storage — all of English Wikipedia is about 110 GB with pictures, 57 GB without (November 2024).
  3. Print or save official emergency guidance as PDF separately.

Meshtastic WMM rating: Conditional · on hold until device and radio certification are checked

WhatA text network built with dedicated radios — the app alone does nothing
DevicesA LoRa radio + a phone paired by Bluetooth (Android or iPhone)
You needOne LoRa device per person, region set to 'KR' (920–923 MHz) in Korea
Works offlineTexts between people who have devices; more devices relay messages further
Does not work offlinePeople without a device, internet services, reaching 119 or other rescue services
RangeSeveral km in open terrain, much less among buildings or hills
BatteryThe device needs its own battery and charging
CostFree software · devices must be bought (WMM does not buy or recommend one)

Before a disaster

  1. Before buying, ask the seller whether the device has Korean conformity certification (KC) — WMM has not confirmed this.
  2. Set every device to region 'KR' and the same channel.
  3. Test indoors, outdoors and over 1 km.

Actions and preparation by hazard

War, air raids, long disruption

On an alert, go to a designated shelter or solid indoor space. Check routes from home, work and school in advance, and avoid basements that can flood.

30 days72-hour bag, 30-day rotating food, walk the evacuation route
60 daysTemporary housing, transport, water and prescription refills in a safe area
90 daysSplit living, income loss, childcare and schooling plans
1 yearDocument renewal, long-term housing, health and livelihood recovery, regular drills

Ordinary dust masks do not stop chemical gases. Follow the local response agency, since actions depend on the chemical, wind and terrain.

[2] Korea — emergency and air-raid actions

Famine, food supply disruption

Famine is a crisis of food, water, health and income that home stocks alone cannot solve. Find public meals, rations and welfare services before food runs out.

30 daysTry a 7-day menu; set 30-day rotating stock and living costs
60 daysTwo or more suppliers, public aid eligibility, safe transport
90 daysCheck 60–90 days of balanced food, cooking water and storage
1 yearAnnual food budget, income replacement, local supply cooperation

Unknown wild plants or mushrooms, mouldy grain and over-diluted formula are not solutions. People who have not eaten for long need medical assessment before large meals.

[20] WFP — understanding famine

Epidemics

No single method fits every disease. Prepare by route (air and contact, water and hygiene, mosquitoes) and follow current health-authority guidance on isolation, vaccines and treatment.

30 daysHome care plan, safe ventilation, food delivery, steady medicines
60 daysCarer shifts, home life, income loss and resupply routes
90 daysFilters, hygiene supplies, carer load, missed check-ups
1 yearRe-assess vaccines, chronic care and recovery plans

Do not self-medicate with antibiotics, antivirals or disinfectants. Replace masks by condition and contamination, not by a fixed number of days.

[9] CDC — respiratory virus prevention [10] CDC — respiratory viruses and air quality

Volcanoes, ash

In pyroclastic-flow, lava or lahar zones, evacuation comes first. Where ash falls, protect indoor air, and check lahar maps along valleys and rivers.

30 daysRoutes, protective gear, first indoor supplies
60 daysVehicle and ventilation filters, alternative housing
90 daysWater, power, housing and care during prolonged ash
1 yearRe-assess living in the zone, building condition, livelihood

N95 masks filter particles, not volcanic gas. Do not climb onto roofs to clear ash; ask experts about load and collapse risk.

[14] USGS — volcanic ash protection [15] USGS — prepare for volcanic activity

Earthquakes, aftershocks

While it shakes, drop, cover your head and neck and hold on. When it stops, check damage, fire, gas, tsunami risk at the coast and evacuation orders.

30 daysSecure furniture, family drill, bag
60 daysWater and power cuts, temporary housing
90 daysDamage checks, repairs, medical and insurance records
1 yearBuilding safety review, drills, insurance terms

Do not rely on doorframes or the 'triangle of life' claim.

[11] CDC — during an earthquake [12] CDC — earthquake preparedness

Tsunamis

Strong or long shaking at the coast, a sudden sea change or a roar from the ocean are natural warnings. After protecting yourself, go to high ground or inland without waiting for an alert.

30 daysWalk the route; adjust bag location
60 daysPractise blocked roads, night, separated family
90 daysShelter living, documents, insurance, housing recovery
1 yearRe-check maps and routes when travelling or moving

Do not return after the first wave. Wait for the all-clear, and avoid plans that depend on driving through traffic.

[13] NOAA — during a tsunami

Wildfires, smoke

If fire approaches and you are told to leave, leave early. If only smoke comes in and the building is safe, set up a clean-air room; move if it gets too hot or breathing worsens.

30 daysBag, clean-air room, backup lodging
60 daysFilters, supplies, power and cooling options
90 daysAdjust housing, school, work and care in long smoke
1 yearBefore fire season: routes, surroundings, insurance

Air purifiers and N95 masks do not supply oxygen or remove carbon monoxide. Never stay because of indoor filtration when fire approaches.

[16] AirNow — be smoke ready [17] AirNow — when smoke is in the air

Comets, asteroids, meteors

No personal gear can stop an impact. Rely on NASA, recognised astronomers and local agencies, and apply blast, fire, tsunami or outage actions as advised. This page predicts no impact.

30 daysKnow official sources; finish basic preparedness
60 daysCheck misinformation steps and family contacts
90 daysLodging, health and documents for wide-area moves
1 yearUpdate the general plan; avoid unfounded overbuying

No one should calculate impact dates or claim a year's stock guarantees survival.

[21] NASA — planetary defense [22] NASA CNEOS — impact exercises

Nuclear, radioactive fallout

Get inside the nearest solid building, in a basement or the centre away from walls and roof, and listen for official instructions. If you were outside, remove outer clothing into a bag and wash exposed skin.

30 daysIndoor shelter space, radio, basic bag
60 daysFamily contact plan, minimise outdoor time
90 daysLodging, health and documents for long sheltering
1 yearRe-check official channels and drills

CDC's 'stay inside at least 24 hours' is general protection, not an all-clear. Particle masks do not block external radiation.

[19] CDC — radiation emergencies

Floods, heavy rain, typhoons

When a warning is issued, check when it reaches your area and leave low ground, flood-prone areas, underground spaces, riverbanks and landslide slopes early. Never walk or drive into flood water.

30 daysEvacuation site and route, 3-day supplies, drains checked
60 daysSandbags and flood boards before the rainy season, a plan for moving cars
90 daysHow to disinfect, check wiring and keep documents after a flood
1 yearEach year before the rainy season, re-check local hazard maps and drills

15 cm (6 in) of fast water can knock an adult over and 30 cm (12 in) can carry most cars away. After water recedes there is still risk of electric shock, contamination and collapse, so wait for the all-clear.

[24] Korea — typhoon actions [25] Korea — heavy rain actions [26] NWS — Turn Around Don't Drown [31] CDC — flood safety

Extreme heat

When heat is forecast, cut outdoor activity, drink water often and check on neighbours who are vulnerable, such as older people living alone. With dizziness, nausea, headache or cramps, rest somewhere cool; if consciousness changes, call emergency services at once.

30 daysCooling checked, cooling centres located, contact list for vulnerable relatives
60 daysShade, water and electrolytes before summer
90 daysWhere to go and how, if power fails in the heat
1 yearEach summer, update how you check on vulnerable relatives and neighbours

Caffeine and alcohol do not help in heat. Follow your local heat warnings; in Korea an advisory is issued when highs of 33 °C or more last two days, and a warning at 35 °C.

[27] Korea — heatwave actions [28] NWS — heat safety

Cold waves, heavy snow

When a cold wave is forecast, limit going out and cover up with thermal layers, a scarf, hat and gloves. Severe shivering, memory loss, disorientation, slurred speech or exhaustion may mean hypothermia — get medical help at once.

30 daysHeating and pipes checked, warm clothes and blankets, outage lighting
60 daysBefore winter, frost-proofing and car winter kit
90 daysA warm place to stay if heating fails, and how to get there
1 yearEach winter, check CO alarms and heater safety

Running a car heater with the windows shut makes drivers drowsy; air the car at least every 30 minutes. People with diabetes or chronic lung disease should get a flu vaccine in advance.

[29] Korea — cold wave actions [30] CDC — winter weather safety [18] CDC — safety after disasters, carbon monoxide

Long power outages

In a long outage, chilled food, medical devices, heating or cooling and communication fail together. A closed fridge keeps food about 4 hours and a full freezer about 48; never run generators or charcoal and gas appliances indoors.

30 daysLighting, batteries, radio; know the food safety times
60 daysBackup power for medical devices; test offline tools
90 daysWhere to stay and how to reach people if heating or cooling stops
1 yearA yearly outage drill and battery replacement

Carbon monoxide has no smell. Run generators only outside, away from windows and doors; with headache, dizziness or nausea, get to fresh air and call emergency services.

[5] CDC — food safety after an emergency [18] CDC — safety after disasters, carbon monoxide [27] Korea — heatwave actions [29] Korea — cold wave actions

Infants, older people, disability, chronic illness and pets need individual plans. Discuss spare prescriptions, cold-chain medicines and backup power for medical devices with a clinician. [6] CDC — prescriptions [7] FDA — safe drug use after a disaster [8] CDC — infant feeding in emergencies
