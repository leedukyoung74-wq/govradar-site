What governments tell people to prepare for

Across war, disasters, epidemics and cyberattacks, the Pentagon wants bases able to hold out 90 days, and Europe, the UK, Japan and Canada want households ready for three days to a week. Here are the announcements as headline cards.

Cards are our own summaries styled as front pages, not screenshots (outlets' photos and layouts are copyrighted). Tap a card for the original. As of 2026-11-15

How long are people told to hold out?

Most household advice is 3 days; the Nordics and Japan say a week, Germany up to 10 days. The US military aims for 90 days per base.

US DoD — major bases90 days
Germanyup to 10 days
Sweden · Norwayup to 7 days
Japan3–7 days
EU · France · UK · Canada · Netherlands · Denmark · Belgium3 days (72 h)
Korea (existing guidance)2–3 day go-bag

🇪🇺 European Commission · 2026.11.03

Presented at the European Civil Protection Forum in Brussels. Households should keep an emergency bag against extreme weather, cyberattacks, sabotage of infrastructure and geopolitical conflict; 72 hours lets emergency services mobilise for those most in need. Member states should teach preparedness in early schooling and include people with disabilities in plans.

Checklist · ~6 L water per person · non-perishable food · medicines · hygiene items · emergency cash

🇯🇵 Japan (new Disaster Prevention Agency) · 2026.11.02

Timed to the agency's launch on 2 Nov, the request goes to citizens, local governments and ministries: water, food and portable toilets for at least three days, ideally a week, and furniture secured. It restates the 2026 white paper; officials say it is not a response to any specific disaster or cyberattack. Background: Nankai Trough and Tokyo quake risk.

Checklist · Water, food and portable toilets for 3–7 days · secure furniture

🇺🇸 US Department of Defense · 2026.10.25

Unveiled by the Defense Secretary on 30 Sep, with a program office ordered within 30 days. Planning documents call for major CONUS installations to sustain essential operations for at least 90 days without outside support: power, water, fuel and communications. Includes microreactors, large batteries, microgrids, cyber and drone defence, supply chains for munitions, medicine and blood, and domestic rare earths and chips. No cost or timeline yet; full rollout across ~500 bases could take decades.
